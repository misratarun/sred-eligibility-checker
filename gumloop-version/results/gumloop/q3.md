3. CRITERIA ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

Criterion 1: TECHNOLOGICAL UNCERTAINTY
─────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ❌ NOT MET

Justification:
The CRA defines technological uncertainty as: "It is unknown (or uncertain) 
whether a given result or objective can be achieved, due to an insufficiency in 
the available scientific or technological knowledge."

Your work applied known database optimization techniques (window functions, 
materialized CTEs, custom indexing, memory tuning). These are well-documented 
features in PostgreSQL and standard practice in database optimization.

The one "non-obvious join reordering" you mention may reflect a specific tuning 
decision, but does not constitute uncertainty about *whether* the approach would 
work. You were applying query planner knowledge to a specific cost-based problem, 
not resolving whether the technique itself was viable.

Citation: "Guidelines on the eligibility of work for scientific research and 
experimental development (SR&ED) tax incentives - Canada.ca.pdf" 
(Technological Uncertainty section, p. 3)
"It is needed when it is unknown (or uncertain) whether a given result or 
objective can be achieved, due to an insufficiency in the available scientific 
or technological knowledge."


Criterion 2: SYSTEMATIC INVESTIGATION
─────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ⚠ PARTIALLY MET (but insufficient on its own)

Justification:
You did conduct systematic work: benchmarking, analysis using EXPLAIN plans, 
iterative tuning. This demonstrates a methodical approach.

However, the CRA distinguishes between "a systematic approach to carrying out 
work" and "a systematic investigation or search" as defined in SR&ED:

"Businesses follow known design methods, techniques, procedures, protocols, 
standards and other practices. Problems are often solved using a systematic 
approach that follows established procedures and standards. Nonetheless, it is 
not enough that work be carried out systematically in order for it to be 
eligible for SR&ED tax incentives."

Your systematic approach to optimization is standard operational practice, not 
a systematic investigation into unknown/uncertain technical questions. The 
investigation was *of* your own system's performance, not *of* whether a 
technique would work.

Citation: "Guidelines on the eligibility of work for scientific research and 
experimental development (SR&ED) tax incentives - Canada.ca.pdf" 
(The "How" Requirement section, p. 4)
"It is important to distinguish between a systematic approach to carrying out 
work and the approach that is a systematic investigation or search called for 
in the definition of SR&ED."


Criterion 3: TECHNOLOGICAL ADVANCEMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ❌ NOT MET

Justification:
The CRA requires that work advance "the understanding of science or technology" — 
i.e., generate conceptual, generalizable knowledge, not just data or measurements.

Your optimization improved *your company's system performance*. It did not:
- Advance the general understanding of PostgreSQL optimization
- Generate novel techniques applicable beyond your specific query patterns
- Contribute new knowledge to database systems research
- Publish findings in peer-reviewed venues

The CRA explicitly states:
"The type of knowledge in this context is conceptual knowledge, such as theories 
or prediction models, rather than just factual knowledge, such as data or 
measurements."

And:
"Acquiring available knowledge or know-how, for example, through training, 
on-the-job learning, hiring expert employees or consultants, or by purchasing 
proprietary knowledge does not meet the requirement of attempting to achieve 
technological advancement."

Your work was learning and applying existing database optimization knowledge to 
your own operations, not advancing the field.

Citation: "Guidelines on the eligibility of work for scientific research and 
experimental development (SR&ED) tax incentives - Canada.ca.pdf" 
(The "Why" Requirement section, p. 3; Experimental Development section, p. 7)

4. SUPPORTING EVIDENCE (CRA GUIDELINES)
================================================================================

EXCLUSION: "Quality Control or Routine Testing"

The CRA excludes from SR&ED:
"Quality control or routine testing of materials, devices, products or processes"

Database query optimization and tuning is analogous to quality assurance and 
performance tuning — operational work, not R&D. Your use of EXPLAIN plans and 
benchmarking is standard practice in database administration.

Citation: "Guidelines on the eligibility of work for scientific research and 
experimental development (SR&ED) tax incentives - Canada.ca.pdf" 
(Categories of Ineligible Work, p. 10)

KEY DISTINCTION — Support vs. Excluded Work:

The CRA explains:
"A key feature that can help you distinguish between support work and excluded 
work is purpose. Why was the work done? Was it carried out specifically to 
perform the scientific research or experimental development? Or was it done for 
some other reason?"

Your query optimization was done to improve production database performance — a 
normal business operation, not to support SR&ED work.

Citation: "Guidelines on the eligibility of work for scientific research and 
experimental development (SR&ED) tax incentives - Canada.ca.pdf" 
(Distinguishing between Support and Excluded Work, p. 10)

================================================================================
5. GAPS AND RISKS
================================================================================

Why This Claim Would Likely Be Challenged:

1. **No Research Question**: You optimized queries you already owned and 
   understood. You were not investigating whether a technique would work; you 
   were applying known techniques to your own data.

2. **Standard Practices**: Window functions, CTEs, indexing, and memory tuning 
   are documented PostgreSQL features. There is no uncertainty about their 
   availability or basic behavior.

3. **No Advancement Beyond Your System**: The improvements are specific to your 
   query patterns and data. They do not generalize or advance database systems 
   knowledge.

4. **The "Non-Obvious" Join Reordering**: Even if this was non-obvious to your 
   team, the CRA would ask: was it non-obvious because the technique itself was 
   unknown, or because understanding your specific query execution plan required 
   expert tuning? The latter is not SR&ED; it's operational expertise.

5. **No Documentation of Failed Approaches**: You did not provide evidence of:
   - Hypotheses tested
   - Approaches that failed and why
   - Experiments designed to test uncertainty
   - Knowledge gaps that blocked progress

   These would be necessary to argue a "systematic investigation" of an 
   uncertain problem.

6. RECOMMENDATION
================================================================================

This project should NOT be claimed as SR&ED. Including it in an SR&ED claim 
would risk audit scrutiny because:
- It fits the CRA's definition of excluded/routine work
- Your own description identifies it as "standard tuning practice"
- It clearly lacks the core "Why" component (advancement of understanding)

IF YOU WANT TO MAKE IT ELIGIBLE:

You would need to reframe the project around a genuine research question, such as:

**HYPOTHETICAL REWORK (not your current project):**

"We investigated whether a novel cost model for join reordering could reduce 
query latency by predicting edge cases in PostgreSQL's query planner. We 
designed experiments to:
1. Test whether the planner's heuristics fail on non-standard table sizes
2. Propose and test alternative join orderings
3. Measure whether our predictions generalize to other query patterns

This would generate new knowledge about query optimization and potentially 
contribute to PostgreSQL's understanding of join selection."

But this is NOT what you described. Your work was optimization, not research.

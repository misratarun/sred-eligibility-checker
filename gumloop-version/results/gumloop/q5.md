SR&ED ELIGIBILITY ASSESSMENT: DBSCAN ADAPTATION FOR TIME-SERIES ANOMALY DETECTION
================================================================================

**PROJECT SUMMARY**
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
You adapted the DBSCAN clustering algorithm (originally designed for spatial 
data) to detect anomalous behavior patterns in time-series network traffic logs.

Core Challenge: DBSCAN operates on Euclidean distances in geometric space. Your 
data is high-dimensional, non-Euclidean event sequences, requiring a custom 
distance metric combining edit distance and temporal weighting.

Work Scope:
  • Core algorithm: DBSCAN (well understood)
  • Novel adaptation: Time-series, high-dimensional, non-Euclidean domain
  • Custom distance metric: 4 variants tested over 2 months
  • Results: Works for your dataset, generalization unknown


================================================================================
1. CLASSIFICATION: BORDERLINE (LIKELY ELIGIBLE WITH CONDITIONS)
================================================================================

This project sits at the boundary between operational AI engineering and 
legitimate SR&ED. It *could* qualify, but documentation and evidence gaps 
must be resolved.

Confidence: MEDIUM

The technical novelty (DBSCAN + custom metric for time-series) and the 
explicit uncertainty about generalization are genuine. However, the final 
verdict depends on whether you can demonstrate:
  1. Documented hypotheses and rationale for the four metric variants
  2. Systematic experimentation (controls, metrics, validation methodology)
  3. Intent to solve a general problem vs. a one-off business problem

2. CRITERIA ASSESSMENT
================================================================================

Criterion 1: TECHNOLOGICAL UNCERTAINTY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ✅ LIKELY MET (with evidence gaps)

Justification:

The CRA defines technological uncertainty as: "It is unknown (or uncertain) 
whether a given result or objective can be achieved, due to an insufficiency 
in the available scientific or technological knowledge."
(Reference: CRA SR&ED Glossary)

Your work exhibits genuine uncertainty:

  ✓ DBSCAN is not documented for non-Euclidean, high-dimensional time-series
    The algorithm was designed for spatial clustering, not event sequences.

  ✓ Standard distance metrics (Euclidean, Manhattan) do not work on your data
    Your domain (network traffic events) does not map cleanly to geometric space.

  ✓ Custom metric design was not obvious
    You needed to combine edit distance (for sequence structure) with temporal
    weighting (for time sensitivity). This is not a standard recipe in the ML
    literature for this specific problem.

  ✓ EXPLICIT UNCERTAINTY STATED: "we are uncertain whether the distance metric
    generalizes beyond our specific traffic patterns"
    This is not "we haven't tested it yet." This is "after investigation, we
    still don't know if it generalizes." That is textbook technological
    uncertainty.

HOWEVER — Risk Flags:

  ⚠️  "Works for our dataset" is too narrow
    If the solution is tightly fitted to your specific traffic patterns, it
    may not constitute an advancement of general knowledge. The CRA expects
    SR&ED to generate insights that *could* apply beyond the immediate problem.

  ⚠️  Unclear whether you tried to *understand* the generalization gap
    Did you:
      • Test the metric on other datasets (even synthetic)?
      • Analyze why it fails to generalize (if it does)?
      • Document the boundary conditions?
    OR did you simply acknowledge "it works here, might not elsewhere" and move on?

    If the latter, that's application engineering. If the former, that's
    research.

Criterion 2: SYSTEMATIC INVESTIGATION
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ⚠️  PARTIALLY MET (needs detail)

Justification:

You mention "four distance function variants tested over two months." That
is structured work. But the CRA requires more than "we tried multiple
approaches." You need evidence of:

  1. HYPOTHESIS-DRIVEN DESIGN
     Did you formulate specific hypotheses about EACH variant?

     Example (strong evidence):
       "Variant 1: Edit distance alone. Hypothesis: sequence structure
        matters more than timing. Prediction: high precision, poor recall.
        Result: Confirmed. Precision 91%, Recall 64%."

     Example (weak evidence):
       "We tried 4 variants. One worked better."

  2. DOCUMENTATION OF RATIONALE
     For each variant, can you explain:
       • What knowledge gap motivated this specific design?
       • How does this variant differ from the previous one?
       • What did you expect to change (and why)?

  3. RIGOROUS EVALUATION
     Did you use:
       • Consistent evaluation metrics across variants?
       • Cross-validation or held-out test sets?
       • Statistical significance testing?
       • Analysis of failure modes?

     OR did you use:
       • "It worked" / "It didn't work"?
       • Single-run results?
       • Anecdotal success on your dataset?

  4. DOCUMENTED ITERATION CYCLE
     The CRA looks for evidence of analysis leading to new hypotheses:
       Observation → Analysis → Hypothesis → Experiment → New Observation

     Not just:
       Try → Fail/Succeed → Try Again

Currently: UNKNOWN. Your description mentions systematic testing (4 variants,
2 months), which is promising. But without documentation showing hypothesis
design, controlled evaluation, and decision rationale, this could be seen as
"algorithm tuning" rather than "systematic investigation of a technological
uncertainty."

Criterion 3: TECHNOLOGICAL ADVANCEMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Status: ❓ UNCERTAIN (scope-dependent)

Justification:

CRA advancement = "generation or discovery of knowledge that advances the 
understanding of science."

Two interpretations:

INTERPRETATION A: GENERAL ADVANCE (ELIGIBLE)
  Your work advances the field's understanding of:
    • How to design distance metrics for event-sequence clustering
    • Conditions under which DBSCAN can be adapted to non-Euclidean domains
    • Trade-offs between edit distance and temporal weighting

  If you can show that OTHER teams working on similar problems (network
  anomaly detection, event-stream clustering, etc.) could benefit from your
  findings, this qualifies.

  Evidence needed:
    • Can you articulate a generalizable principle learned from your work?
    • Could someone else (not at your company) apply your findings to a
      similar domain?
    • Have you documented your approach in a way that could be shared?

INTERPRETATION B: SPECIFIC SOLUTION (NOT ELIGIBLE)
  Your work solves YOUR company's network anomaly detection problem using
  an ad-hoc combination of known techniques (DBSCAN + edit distance).

  If the solution is purely engineered to fit your data (overfitted), and no
  generalizable insights are transferable, the CRA would classify this as
  "commercial production of a solution" rather than "advancement of knowledge."

  The CRA explicitly excludes:
    "the commercial production of a new or improved material, device or product
     or the commercial use of a new or improved process"
    (Reference: CRA Guidelines, Exclusions section)

RED FLAG: You stated "works for our dataset but we are uncertain whether the 
distance metric generalizes beyond our specific traffic patterns."

This is a MAJOR warning. If the metric doesn't generalize, you may not have
advanced general knowledge — you may have just engineered a good solution for
one specific problem.

QUESTION FOR YOU:
  Do you believe a colleague at another company, working on an event-stream
  clustering problem, could learn something valuable from your distance metric
  design that they couldn't get from existing published literature?

  If YES → Advancement (LIKELY ELIGIBLE)
  If NO → Engineering solution (NOT ELIGIBLE)

4. GAPS & RISKS
================================================================================

CRITICAL GAPS:

  1. GENERALIZATION BOUNDARY UNDEFINED
     You acknowledge uncertainty about whether the metric generalizes. This
     is honest, but it's ALSO a red flag for SR&ED eligibility.

     If the metric doesn't generalize, the CRA will argue:
       → You solved a specific business problem, not a research question.
       → No general knowledge was advanced.
       → Exclusion applies: "commercial use of a new or improved process."

     MITIGATION NEEDED:
       • Test the metric on other datasets (at least synthetic).
       • Document the boundary conditions (where it works, where it fails).
       • Articulate what you learned about event-stream distance metrics
         that is transferable.

  2. HYPOTHESIS DOCUMENTATION MISSING
     You mention "four variants" but no documented hypothesis for each.

     Without this, the CRA could argue:
       → You were "tuning" an algorithm to fit your data.
       → No systematic investigation of uncertainty.
       → Standard engineering practice.

     MITIGATION NEEDED:
       • Reconstruct the hypothesis for each variant (if not documented).
       • Show what you expected each variant to reveal.
       • Document why you chose each next variant based on prior results.

  3. EVALUATION METHODOLOGY UNCLEAR
     You don't mention:
       • How you measured performance (accuracy? precision/recall? anomaly
         detection rate?)
       • Whether you used test sets separate from tuning data.
       • Whether you validated across multiple time periods or traffic
         scenarios.
       • Statistical significance of differences between variants.

     MITIGATION NEEDED:
       • Provide evaluation metrics for each variant.
       • Show you used train/test splits or cross-validation.
       • Demonstrate the evaluation was rigorous (not just "this one works better").

  4. INTENT UNCLEAR: GENERAL vs. SPECIFIC
     The biggest threat to eligibility:
       If the work was intended to solve YOUR NETWORK PROBLEM (specific),
       then it's NOT SR&ED.

       If the work was intended to advance understanding of DISTANCE METRICS
       FOR EVENT SEQUENCES (general), then it IS SR&ED.

     MITIGATION NEEDED:
       • Clarify: Was the primary goal "detect anomalies in our traffic" or
         "understand how to design metrics for event-stream clustering"?
       • If goal was general, document insights learned.
       • If goal was specific, reclassify as operational engineering.

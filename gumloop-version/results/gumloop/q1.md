5. GAPS OR RISKS
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DOCUMENTATION GAPS (relative risk: LOW to MEDIUM):

  ⚠ Hypothesis logs detail: Are logs detailed enough to show WHY each
    hypothesis was tested, what was expected vs. observed, and what failed?
    CRA values clear records of failed experiments and the reasoning behind
    design iterations. If logs are cursory ("tried depth 5, then depth 6"),
    that weakens the claim.

  ⚠ Baseline justification: Is it clear that the 31% comparison is against
    state-of-the-art published methods, not straw-man implementations? CRA
    reviewers will scrutinize whether baselines were credible and properly
    configured. Provide citations or clear descriptions of each baseline's
    known performance in peer literature.

  ⚠ Ablation study documentation: Are the ablation results clearly isolated
    (e.g., "message-passing depth contribution: +X%", "attention head
    contribution: +Y%")? This demonstrates that design choices were driven by
    evidence, not intuition.

  ⚠ Dynamic load validation: Is it explicit in your documentation that the
    improvement (31%) specifically comes from handling dynamic edge weights,
    not just static graph improvements? CRA may challenge whether the novelty
    is truly in the dynamic aspect or just a better static GNN. Split your
    results to show: (a) static-graph performance vs. baselines, and (b)
    dynamic-graph performance improvement as the gap-closing contribution.

TECHNICAL RISKS (relative risk: LOW):

  • Peer review outcome: If the paper is rejected, that does NOT disqualify the
    work retroactively. CRA's "Why" criterion requires only that your PURPOSE
    was advancement, not that external review confirmed it. However, acceptance
    strengthens the case.

  • Commodity vs. novel: GNN model design is a legitimate field of
    technological advancement. Provided your architecture is a new combination
    (not a direct copy of published papers with minor tuning), you are safe.
    Risk: If CRA argues "you just applied standard GNN techniques," this is
    mitigated by your ablation studies showing why your specific combination
    works differently.
